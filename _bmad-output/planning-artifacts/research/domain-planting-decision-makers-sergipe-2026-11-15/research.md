# Who leads or advises planting decisions in Sergipe's Agreste and Semiárido

**Decision this research serves:** the team chooses the technical persona and the role of AI (E7). This report lists the roles the evidence shows. It **does not rank them, choose one or propose product features**.

## Executive summary

- **Several roles shape the planting decision.** In this quick pass the evidence shows at least five kinds of actor:
  - **public extension technicians** (Emdagro, the state ATER body) [1][2];
  - **Senar's technical and managerial assistance (ATeG)** technicians [4];
  - **technical consultants at input resellers**, who influence the choice of maize hybrids [10];
  - **professionals who write rural credit projects** (usually agronomists) and the **bank analysts** who check them [13][14];
  - **larger maize producers** in the Carira, Simão Dias and Frei Paulo hub [5][6].
- **Rules shape when farmers plant.** To get official rural credit, Proagro or subsidised insurance, the planting date has to fall inside the ZARC window. From Plano Safra 25/26 this also applies to loans above R$200k and to contracts without Proagro [8][9]. One rule (from 2022, to verify) says a Pronaf operation outside ZARC needs an ATER indication to enter Proagro Mais [7].
- **Public technical assistance is scarce.**
  - Nationally, only 20.2% of farm establishments reported receiving technical guidance in 2017 [12].
  - Studies describe a chronic shortage of public extension in the Northeast [15][16].
  - The Sergipe-specific figures were **not found**.
- **Biggest caveat:** this was a quick, desk-only pass. Almost no first-hand voice of these professionals was found: no complaints about their tools, and no time or effort per decision. Most sources are institutional or press. The gaps are listed under open questions.

## 1. Who decides or advises (roles found)

| Role | Who they serve | Evidence | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Emdagro extension technician** (agronomists, agricultural technicians, vets) | Family farmers; 1,732 assisted in 2018, with a goal of 3,000 families by 2020 in the Agreste Central and Centro Sul [2] | Emdagro is the state ATER body since 1991 [1]; it took part in the 2026 maize seed distribution (10 kg per farmer) before the São José planting [11] | medium (headcount not found; figures are old) |
| **Senar ATeG technician** | Producers enrolled in ATeG, mostly dairy (392 in the semiarid in 2021) and shrimp [4] | Free technical and managerial assistance; Senar reports +25% gross income after 2 years [4] | medium (self-reported; little on grain crops) |
| **Input reseller technical consultant** | Maize producers who buy from the reseller | A 2026 UFS thesis interviewed consultants from 12 leading resellers in five municipalities: they influence the hybrid choice, and reseller-assisted areas are reported 88% above the official yield [10] | low-medium (student thesis, abstract only) |
| **Rural credit project writer** (usually an agronomist) **and bank analyst** | Farmers taking Pronaf or other credit | The Pronaf technical study can be a simple plan, a project or an integrated project with technical guidance [13]; BNB screens projects with its "Analisador do PRONAF" and SEAP, and out-of-range projects need a bank technician's opinion [14] | low-medium (verify the current credit manual) |
| **Larger maize producer or farm manager** | Their own farm | About 6,000 maize producers, concentrated in Frei Paulo, Carira and Simão Dias [6]; yields of about 5.8 to 6.0 t/ha [5]; heavy use of hybrid seed and inputs [5][17] | medium |
| **Cooperative agronomist** | Not found | Only a 1980s mention of a Nossa Senhora da Glória colonisation cooperative [3] | **not found** (current) |

**Family farming context:** 72,060 establishments, 77.3% of Sergipe's total, are family farms (Census 2017) [18].

## 2. How the work runs

- **Season:** maize is tied to the São José (March) and São João (June) plantings. The state distributes seed before São José [11].
- **The planting window is regulated:** ZARC compliance is required for credit, Proagro and insurance [8][9]. Windows cannot be changed during the current season [8].
- **Paperwork and approval:** credit needs a technical study [13], and the bank checks its technical indicators [14]. A professional chooses the inputs and the technical parameters before the farmer gets the money.
- **Hybrid choice:** in the maize hub, reseller consultants influence which hybrid is planted [10].
- **Not found:** how long each decision takes, how many farmers each technician serves in Sergipe, and how beans and cassava decisions are made.

## 3. Difficulties and tools

- **Coverage gap:** public ATER does not meet demand in the Northeast; budgets fell after 2016 [15][16]. In Emdagro's 2026 coffee census, no producer had crop-specific assistance [19] (coffee, not a v1 crop).
- **Climate risk:** drought cut the 2026 maize projection in Sergipe, Alagoas and Bahia [20].
- **Tools named:**
  - **ZARC Plantio Certo** (Embrapa): planting dates by municipality and crop for 43 crops, usable by the owner or the technician, free for personal use up to 1,000 ha, built on the Agritec API [21][22];
  - **BNB's Analisador do PRONAF and SEAP** for credit projects [14].
- **Not found:** first-hand complaints from technicians about these tools, weather services or data, and any study of where their decisions fail.

## 4. Vocabulary (terms the sources use)

ATER (technical assistance and rural extension), ATeG (Senar's technical and managerial assistance), ZARC (agricultural climate-risk zoning), janela de plantio (planting window), Pronaf, Proagro / Proagro Mais, Garantia-Safra, projeto técnico / plano simples (credit study), revenda (input reseller), híbrido (hybrid seed), plantio de São José / São João, sequeiro (rain-fed).

## 5. Cross-dimension insights

- **Rules and advice meet at the planting date.** Credit and insurance tie the planting date to ZARC [7][8][9], and public assistance is thin [12][15]. Several actors therefore touch the same decision, each for a different reason (credit, inputs, extension).
- **The maize hub and family farming are different contexts.** The hub around Carira and Simão Dias is input-intensive with reseller assistance [5][10][17]. Most Sergipe establishments are family farms [18], reached mainly by public programs [2][11].
